  • Explaining why leaf thinning or keeping shade changes ripeness

Source vs Flow

Source — recognize a trained vs bush vine and say what a canopy does. Flow — connect a site's water and sun to its training choice and predict which wine style it favors.

Bridge: Row choices echo all the way to Chart 05: growers aiming for traditional-method sparkling often pick earlier for bright acid, while fortified and bold red growers often wait for riper sugar.

Give-Back: Every vineyard decision is a water decision. Learners note one water-saving practice in the row and how a similar idea could help a community well or garden.

02 · White Grape Varieties — Species Analysis

Original Sip Studies teaching. Public variety names. Not affiliated with any court, guild, or credentialing body.

Analyze the core white wine grapes as plants and as styles: who each variety is, where it grows best, how its structure reads, and how to explain it to a guest.

Sip Families (white): Crisp = high acid, light body, little or no oak · Floral = aromatic, perfumed, often lower acid or with sweetness · Rich = fuller body, often oak, ripeness, or lees texture. A variety can move between families depending on climate and winemaking (shown with →).

05 · Wine Making Styles: Red · White · Rosé · Champagne · Fortified

Five wines, five separate paths. Each style makes its own key decisions from crush or press to the vessel — no shared shortcut.

Each style is its own path. Follow one lane from press to vessel, then compare lanes.

The fork · five making styles

  1. Redskins in for color and grip
  2. Whitepress first, keep it fresh
  3. Roséred grapes, brief skin time
  4. Champagnesecond ferment in bottle
  5. Fortifiedgrape spirit added, timing sets sweetness

What you practice

  • Walking each of the five lanes in order without notes
  • Naming the one decision that defines each style (skin time, press speed, rosé method, second ferment in bottle, spirit timing)
  • Explaining why rosé is made from red grapes but handled like a white
  • Telling a guest in one sentence where traditional-method bubbles come from
  • Sorting a fortified wine by spirit timing and aging family

Source vs Flow

Source — name the five styles and the defining step in each lane. Flow — compare two lanes side by side (for example red vs rosé, or white vs Champagne base wine), predict how one decision changes the glass, and recommend a style for a guest with a reason.

Bridge: Lanes hand off to Chart 06, where each style meets its own vessel and aging pattern.

Give-Back: Every lane uses water — cleaning presses, cooling tanks, washing bottles for the second ferment. Learners pick one lane and write its water-saving pledge for a Water to Wine event.
